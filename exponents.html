    1What Is an Exponent?

    An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself. The number being multiplied is the base. Together they make a power.

    2^5 = 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 = 32

    In 2^5, the base is 2 and the exponent is 5. You write out five 2s and multiply them. That is not the same as 2 · 5 = 10.

    RememberA power is repeated multiplication, not multiplying the base by the exponent. 3^4 means 3 · 3 · 3 · 3 = 81, not 3 · 4 = 12.

    Example

    What is 5^3?

    1. 5^3 = 5 · 5 · 5The exponent 3 means three factors of 5.
    2. 5 · 5 = 25Multiply the first two.
    3. 25 · 5 = 125Then multiply by the last 5.

    Watch the parentheses

    (-3)^2 means (-3) · (-3) = 9. The negative is inside, so it gets multiplied too.

    -3^2 means -(3 · 3) = -9. The exponent only applies to the 3. The negative sits outside.

    Common mix-up
    • 10^4 is 1 followed by 4 zeros: 10,000, not 40.
    • Any nonzero number to the first power is itself: 9^1 = 9.

    2Multiplying Powers With the Same Base

    When you multiply two powers that have the same base, keep that base and add the exponents.

    Product rulea^m · a^n = a^{m+n}This only works when the bases match.

    Why it works

    2^3 · 2^4 means three 2s times four 2s:

    1. 2^3 · 2^4 = (2 · 2 · 2) · (2 · 2 · 2 · 2)Write out the factors.
    2. = 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 · 2That’s 7 factors of 2.
    3. = 2^7Add the exponents: 3 + 4 = 7. The base stays 2.

    Example

    Simplify 9^3 · 9^7.

    1. 9^3 · 9^7 = 9^{3+7}Same base of 9, so add the exponents.
    2. = 9^{10}3 + 7 = 10. Do not multiply 3 × 7, and do not change the 9.

    A lonely number is a power too: 7 = 7^1, so 7 · 7^4 = 7^1 · 7^4 = 7^5.

    Same base only
    • 2^3 · 2^4 = 2^7, not 4^7 and not 2^{12}.
    • If the bases are different, you cannot add the exponents. That’s the next section’s big idea, and it shows up a lot in class.

    3Different Bases Stay Different

    This is one of the most important ideas in your class right now. Look at something like:

    9^3 · 10^7

    The bases are 9 and 10. Those are different numbers, so you cannot smash this into one power by adding or multiplying the exponents.

    The ruleYou may combine exponents only when the bases are the same. Different bases stay as separate powers.

    What you should NOT do

    1. 9^3 · 10^7Do not add: this is not 9^{10} or 10^{10}.
    2. 9^3 · 10^7Do not multiply the exponents: this is not 9^{21} or 10^{21}. Multiplying exponents is for a power of a power, like (a^7)^2.
    3. 9^3 · 10^7Do not multiply the bases and keep one exponent: this is not 90^{10} or 90^3.

    What you CAN do

    Leave it as 9^3 · 10^7, or evaluate each power if you need a regular number:

    1. 9^3 = 9 · 9 · 9 = 729The 9 has its own exponent.
    2. 10^7 = 10,000,000The 10 has its own exponent. 1 followed by 7 zeros.
    3. 9^3 · 10^7 = 729 · 10,000,000 = 7,290,000,000Multiply the two results if you need the value. The expression 9^3 · 10^7 is already simplified as a product of powers.

    Sometimes different-looking bases are secretly the same. Then you rewrite first:

    Rewrite so the bases match

    Simplify 9^3 · 3^2.

    1. 9 = 3^29 is a power of 3, so rewrite the first base.
    2. 9^3 = (3^2)^3 = 3^{2 · 3} = 3^6Power of a power: multiply the exponents. (More on that next.)
    3. 3^6 · 3^2 = 3^8Now the bases match, so add: 6 + 2 = 8.
    Quick check before you combine
    • Are the bases the same number? Yes → you can add (when multiplying) or subtract (when dividing).
    • Are the bases different, like 9 and 10? Leave them separate.
    • Can you rewrite one base to match the other (like 9 = 3^2 or 8 = 2^3)? Then rewrite first, then combine.

    4Power of a Power

    When a power is raised to another power, multiply the exponents and keep the same base.

    Power of a power(a^m)^n = a^{m · n}Example: if you see something like a 7th power raised to the 2nd, the new exponent is 7 · 2 = 14.

    Why it works

    Simplify (2^7)^2.

    1. (2^7)^2 = 2^7 · 2^7A power of 2 means multiply the inside by itself.
    2. = 2^{7+7} = 2^{14}Same base, so add… which is the same as multiplying 7 × 2.
    3. (2^7)^2 = 2^{14}Shortcut: multiply the exponents. The base stays 2.

    Example

    Simplify (5^3)^4.

    1. (5^3)^4 = 5^{3 · 4}Power of a power: multiply.
    2. = 5^{12}Not 5^7 (that would be adding) and not 5^{81}.

    Power of a product

    An exponent outside parentheses hits every factor inside:

    (ab)^n = a^n · b^n

    So (2 · 5)^3 = 2^3 · 5^3, and (9 · 10)^3 = 9^3 · 10^3. Notice that last one is 9^3 · 10^3, not 9^3 · 10^7 — each factor gets the same outside exponent.

    Don’t mix up the two multiply situations
    • a^7 · a^2 = a^9 — multiplying powers with the same base: add 7 + 2.
    • (a^7)^2 = a^{14} — a power raised to a power: multiply 7 × 2.
    • Look for the parentheses. Parentheses around a power, with another exponent outside, means multiply.

    5Dividing Powers With the Same Base

    When you divide two powers that have the same base, keep that base and subtract the exponents.

    Quotient rulea^m / a^n = a^{m-n}Again: same base only.

    Example

    Simplify 5^7 / 5^3.

    1. 5^7 / 5^3 = 5^{7-3}Same base of 5, so subtract the exponents.
    2. = 5^47 − 3 = 4.

    Why it works

    2^5 / 2^2 = (2 · 2 · 2 · 2 · 2) / (2 · 2). Two of the 2s cancel, leaving three 2s: 2^3.

    If the exponents are equal, everything cancels and you get 1: 3^5 / 3^5 = 3^0 = 1. (Zero exponents are next.)

    Different bases still stay apart
    • 9^7 / 10^3 cannot become 9^4 or 10^4. The bases don’t match.
    • You can write it as a fraction, 9^7 / 10^3, or evaluate each power separately.
    ← BackNext: zero and negative exponents →

    6Zero and Negative Exponents

    Any nonzero number to the zero power is 1.

    a^0 = 1 (as long as a is not 0)

    That matches the divide rule: 5^3 / 5^3 = 5^{3-3} = 5^0, and anything divided by itself is 1.

    A negative exponent means “take the reciprocal, then make the exponent positive.” It does not make the answer negative.

    Negative exponenta^{-n} = 1 / a^nSo 2^{-3} = 1 / 2^3 = 1/8, not −8 and not −2^3.

    Example

    Simplify 10^{-3}.

    1. 10^{-3} = 1 / 10^3Negative exponent → flip to the denominator.
    2. 10^3 = 10001 followed by 3 zeros.
    3. = 1/1000 = 0.001You can type 1/1000 or 0.001.

    Example

    Simplify 5^{-2}.

    1. 5^{-2} = 1 / 5^2Flip, then the exponent becomes positive 2.
    2. 5^2 = 255 · 5 = 25.
    3. = 1/25That’s the simplified value.
    The minus is not a “make it negative” sign
    • 3^{-2} = 1/9, which is positive.
    • (-4)^0 = 1. The base is −4, and any nonzero number to the 0 power is 1.

    7Scientific Notation

    Scientific notation writes a number as a decimal times a power of 10:

    a · 10^n

    The first number a must be at least 1 and less than 10 (like 3.4 or 7, but not 34 or 0.34). The exponent on 10 tells you how the decimal point moves.

    Moving the decimalA positive exponent makes a large number: move the decimal right. A negative exponent makes a small number: move the decimal left.

    Large number

    3.4 · 10^4

    1. 3.4Start here. The exponent is +4, so move the decimal 4 places right.
    2. 340003.4 → 34 → 340 → 3400 → 34,000. Fill empty spots with zeros.

    Small number

    4.2 · 10^{-2}

    1. 4.2The exponent is −2, so move the decimal 2 places left.
    2. 0.0424.2 → 0.42 → 0.042.

    Writing a number in scientific notation

    Write 56,000 as a · 10^n.

    1. 5.6Move the decimal so one digit sits to the left of it: 5.6.
    2. 56000 → 5.6You moved the decimal 4 places left, so the power is 10^4.
    3. 56000 = 5.6 · 10^4Check: 5.6 times 10,000 is 56,000.
